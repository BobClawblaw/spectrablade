# Roadmap

What comes next, in order. Finished work moves to the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md).

**Now: the 0.15 line, instruments for sound design.** Feature work stopped at 0.14.7 for dogfooding, and has started again on main for one line of work: instrument plug-ins (VST3, and Audio Units on the Mac) played from notes, patterns and sequences, with their parameters automated, so new sounds can be made from an instrument and its settings and then edited like any other audio. Not a DAW: no tempo map beyond the one the multitrack has, no bars-and-beats sequencing of a song, no MIDI routing between tracks. The first two steps are in (Milestone 362: Generate from Instrument, with the built-in Basic Synth; Milestone 363: instrument tracks, with note clips that hold notes with pitch, velocity, start and length, once or repeating, undoable and saved in the session file, typed into a dialog for now); the rest, in order:

1. **A clip editor:** a piano-roll grid on a note clip with step entry, notes dragged in pitch and time, and a one-note trigger clip for hits; note clips trimmed, split, copied and pasted, and carried with copied time, as audio clips are.
2. **Live input:** MIDI keyboards through the audio device settings, an on-screen keyboard, recording into a clip, and Standard MIDI File import.
3. **Controller lanes:** pitch bend, the mod wheel and CCs as envelopes on a note clip, for the parameters instruments expose that way.

The releases meanwhile are still dogfooding releases, and what real use turns up still goes into the [dogfooding log](docs/DOGFOODING.md) and its [issues](https://github.com/BobClawblaw/spectrablade/issues). 1.0.0 follows once the instrument line has had a round of use with nothing left in the log. The items below wait behind that.

**Done, not on this list:** the mastering suite and the audio-repair suite are in (milestones 297 to 326: every job the big suites do, built our own way, listed in the changelog), as are the Sound Library, surround, video, projects, batch processing, the stretch methods and the rest. What is left of them is use: nothing from milestones 297 to 326 has been listened to yet, and each of those tools has been run once on four real recordings and no more. Open from those runs: a room full of voices is noise to Adaptive Noise Reduction and Dialogue Isolation, which need quiet moments or one pitch (both say so when they find almost nothing to keep); and a rough character voice reads as voiced in 46 % of its loud frames at the middle sensitivity, the rest having two harmonics over the floor or periods unlike each other (taking two harmonics for a voice took hum for a voice too, and was dropped). Both go to the dogfooding log if real use runs into them.

1. **Sound Library category suggestions:** most libraries don't name their files the UCS way, so a category could be suggested for the rest (never taken as the sound's category until accepted). Not built, because what has been tried isn't good enough to offer. Measured on one large library, each vendor's UCS-named sounds guessed from the others': scoring UCS's own words and synonyms against a sound's name and folders got the category right for 0 to 63% of most vendors' sounds; learning from the library's own UCS-named sounds as well (naive Bayes over name and folder words, UCS's words as the prior) got 40% of categories and 17% of exact subcategories, and its confidence didn't single out the right ones (44% right among the half it was surest of). The sounds whose names follow UCS come from only six vendors, 85% of them from one (mostly footsteps), so there is little to learn from. Suggesting from a folder's other sounds was measured too and reaches almost nothing that is really UCS: of the 646,200 sounds there without a category, 31,154 share a folder with sounds that have one, and nearly all of those folders' categorised sounds are ordinary all-caps names that happen to start with a general CatID (WIND_LTL2-0988, RAIN_LIGHT_TO_HEAVY_CS); the folders named the UCS way are named so throughout. Such all-caps names can't be told from UCS names by their shape (requiring UCS's four fields would drop 108 real UCS names, ROBTVox_Mechanimal 070_F0X among them, and keep about as many ordinary ones), so about 150 of that library's sounds still take WIND, RAIN or BEEPS from their names. Next to try: words from the audio itself.
