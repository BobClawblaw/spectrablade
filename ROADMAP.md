# Roadmap

What comes next, in order. Finished work moves to the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md).

Mastering and repair: every job a full mastering suite and a full audio-repair suite do, built our own way (clean-room: the same jobs, with our own methods, names, presets and look; nothing taken from another product's code, presets or help). Every job on that list is now in (milestones 297 to 318). Here: parametric (with a linear-phase mode), dynamic, graphic and match EQ (the parametric and dynamic bands on the mid, the side, the left or the right), compressor, multiband compressor, true-peak limiter and maximizer, de-esser, multiband stereo width, exciter, transient shaper, noise reduction from a noise print or adaptive, dialogue isolation, reduce reverb, click repair (with mouth de-click and de-crackle), de-plosive, breath control, wind, rustle and bleed reduction, wow and flutter, spectral recovery, ambience match, hum removal, de-clip, repair, spectral attenuate/remove/heal, rebalance, centre channel, phase correction, azimuth, stretch and pitch, pitch correction, dialogue contour, level speech, match loudness and delivery specs, dither, a tonal balance panel, codec preview. What is left is making each better on real material: the repair jobs (milestone 319) and the mastering jobs (milestone 320) have each been run once on four real recordings, which found and fixed six things the synthetic tests had not shown, and nothing has been listened to yet. The band split behind the five multiband effects, which turned phase and so lifted the peaks of a limited or clipped sound by 6 to 8 dB, can now run in linear phase (milestone 321), which holds them at the cost of the linear-phase EQ's delay. Open from those runs: a room full of voices defeats Adaptive Noise Reduction and Dialogue Isolation, which need quiet moments or one pitch; a rough character voice reads as voiced in 46 % of its loud frames at the middle sensitivity (39 % before milestone 325 gave its lowest passages their own pitch); the rest have two harmonics over the floor or periods unlike each other.


Sound Library: a browser for a large sound-effects library (100k to a million files and more), with drag and drop into the app and out of it.

1. **Category suggestions:** most libraries don't name their files the UCS way, so a category could be suggested for the rest (never taken as the sound's category until accepted). Not built, because what has been tried isn't good enough to offer. Measured on one large library, each vendor's UCS-named sounds guessed from the others': scoring UCS's own words and synonyms against a sound's name and folders got the category right for 0 to 63% of most vendors' sounds; learning from the library's own UCS-named sounds as well (naive Bayes over name and folder words, UCS's words as the prior) got 40% of categories and 17% of exact subcategories, and its confidence didn't single out the right ones (44% right among the half it was surest of). The sounds whose names follow UCS come from only six vendors, 85% of them from one (mostly footsteps), so there is little to learn from. Suggesting from a folder's other sounds was measured too and reaches almost nothing that is really UCS: of the 646,200 sounds there without a category, 31,154 share a folder with sounds that have one, and nearly all of those folders' categorised sounds are ordinary all-caps names that happen to start with a general CatID (WIND_LTL2-0988, RAIN_LIGHT_TO_HEAVY_CS); the folders named the UCS way are named so throughout. Such all-caps names can't be told from UCS names by their shape (requiring UCS's four fields would drop 108 real UCS names, ROBTVox_Mechanimal 070_F0X among them, and keep about as many ordinary ones), so about 150 of that library's sounds still take WIND, RAIN or BEEPS from their names. Next to try: words from the audio itself.

Not code: nothing from milestones 297 to 325 has been listened to yet, and a Developer ID (for notarizing) is the remaining step before installing opens without Privacy & Security.
